Josh Allen's arm won this game in the fourth quarter, and Trevor Lawrence's mistake at the end sealed it.

Buffalo's passing attack was the engine all day, but it mattered most late. Down four midway through the fourth, Allen found Dalton Kincaid for a 15-yard touchdown against a zone look that gave up the middle of the field, tying the game at the goal line. Then, needing a answer after Jacksonville had scored, Allen uncorked a 36-yard shot to Brandon Cooks down the left sideline � a throw released in a flash and delivered accurately even though he was hit as he let it go, dragging the offense from its own territory into scoring range. Earlier, a similar deep shot to Keon Coleman for 36 yards against a light box had already shown that Buffalo could hurt Jacksonville over the top whenever the safeties gave ground.

Jacksonville, meanwhile, made its living on the ground. Travis Etienne broke off a 26-yard run and a 12-yard conversion on third-and-short, and Bhayshul Tuten added a 13-yard burst late in the first quarter � all with Buffalo showing six in the box and no extra rush, which let Jacksonville's line control things physically for long stretches. That ground success kept the Jaguars competitive even as their pass defense was getting carved up down the stretch.

Buffalo answered the run game with its own power in short yardage. Facing fourth-and-1 twice in the fourth quarter, Allen picked up both � a 4-yard push against a seven-man front and, more importantly, a quarterback sneak that was overturned on review into a 10-yard gain down to the 1, set up behind an extra offensive lineman reported in as eligible. That set up the go-ahead score plunge from a yard out, and the drive before it, a 2-yard sneak on third-and-goal, was part of the same pattern of Buffalo simply finishing every red-zone possession it got.

Add it up and Buffalo's offense was efficient wire to wire � the deep shots, the short-yardage conversions, the finished red-zone drives all point back to the same team executing cleanly when it mattered.

The game's biggest swing came late, though, off Jacksonville's offense rather than Buffalo's. With Jacksonville trailing by three inside the final minute, Lawrence tried to hit a receiver over the middle and instead threw it into the hands of Cole Bishop, who read the route and picked it off � a turnover into a zone look that had numbers to make the play and ended Jacksonville's chance to answer. That interception, layered on top of an earlier first-quarter pick when Lawrence was hit as he threw into a crowded middle of the field, meant Jacksonville kept giving Buffalo the ball back at the moments it could least afford to, even as its running game and touchdown drives to Josh Meyers, Brian Thomas, Parker Washington, and Etienne kept the score within reach until the very end.
