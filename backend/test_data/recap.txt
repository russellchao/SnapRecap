Buffalo won this one because Josh Allen kept finding chunks through the air when it mattered most, and because Buffalo's defense delivered the one takeaway that decided the fourth quarter.

The passing game was the backbone of everything Buffalo did well. Twice Allen hit deep shots down the field against two-deep zone looks that blew the game open — a 36-yard strike to Keon Coleman down the right sideline in the second quarter, and then, deep in the fourth quarter with Buffalo trailing by four, a 36-yard bomb to Brandon Cooks on the left side even with pressure bearing down on him. That Cooks throw set up the go-ahead sequence, and a few plays later Allen found Dalton Kincaid for a touchdown against a three-deep zone to cap the drive. Those three plays were the difference-makers in Buffalo's night through the air, with the pressured completion to Cooks standing out as the single biggest one.

On the ground, Jacksonville actually had the better of it in bulk yardage — Travis Etienne broke off a 26-yard run and a 12-yard conversion, and Bhayshul Tuten added a 13-yard burst, all against light boxes with no extra rushers sent. But when the game got tight in short yardage, Buffalo's ground game was the one that delivered touchdowns. Allen's quarterback runs were the difference in the red zone and on money downs — a two-yard scramble around right tackle for a touchdown in the second quarter, and then a pair of fourth-and-1 conversions in the fourth quarter, including a run up the middle that was overturned on review from a touchdown down to the 1, but still set up the score right after. That fourth-and-1 sequence was the single most important stretch of the game for Buffalo's offense.

Put together, Buffalo's ability to finish drives — hitting touchdowns on both the goal-to-go run and the Kincaid catch, converting both fourth-down tries — is what kept them ahead of a Jacksonville offense that had answers of its own. Trevor Lawrence and the Jaguars scored on quick, efficient throws all game: a hitch to Brock Bowers-less but Barnes... actually Bhayshul Thomas for a short touchdown against four-deep coverage, a Parker Washington touchdown against man coverage, another to Washington off a swing pass, and a touchdown to Etienne on a checkdown that turned into 14 yards. Jacksonville kept pace across four different scoring drives, which is exactly why this stayed close throughout.

What tipped it was the fourth-quarter interception. With Jacksonville driving and trailing by three, Lawrence's throw over the middle intended for Jaquez Meyers was picked off by Christian Bishop against a two-deep zone look, snuffing out a potential go-ahead drive. That turnover, paired with Buffalo's finishing touchdowns on the ensuing possessions, is what put the game away. Jacksonville also had an early interception on a post route that Buffalo turned into good field position, and lost a fumble on a kickoff return in the second quarter, but neither swung momentum the way that late pick did. In the end, Buffalo's chunk plays through the air, its execution in short yardage, and one defensive takeaway in the fourth quarter were enough to hold off a Jacksonville offense that never really went away.
