This one came down to Josh Allen and the Buffalo offense answering every Jacksonville punch, right up until the final possession decided it.

The Jaguars actually had success running the ball most of the day. Travis Etienne broke off a 26-yard gain against a light box in the third quarter, ripped off a 12-yard third-down conversion earlier, and Bhayshul Tuten added a 13-yard burst late in the first — Jacksonville was moving the ball on the ground when it wanted to. But none of that mattered as much as what kept happening in crunch moments, where Buffalo simply had the answer every time.

Allen was terrific throwing the ball down the field. He hit Keon Coleman for 36 yards on a deep shot early in the second quarter, then connected with Brandin Cooks for another 36-yard strike late in the fourth despite taking pressure on the throw — both coming against zone looks that gave up the deep shot. That Cooks completion set up a short touchdown to Dalton Kincaid that put Buffalo back in front in the fourth quarter, capping a stretch where Allen and the passing game were clicking against a defense that couldn't disrupt him.

Buffalo's finishing touch inside the red zone was just as important. Allen scored on a goal-line run in the second quarter to put Buffalo ahead, and after a review overturned an apparent fourth-and-1 touchdown deep in Jacksonville territory in the fourth quarter, Buffalo still came away having converted the down, part of a perfect day on fourth down — Allen also picked up a fourth-and-1 earlier in the fourth quarter on a keeper up the middle. Every trip Buffalo took into scoring range turned into points.

Jacksonville kept clawing back, though. Trevor Lawrence found Brian Thomas Jr. for a short touchdown in the second quarter to take the lead, then hit Parker Washington and Etienne on touchdown throws in the fourth quarter to answer Buffalo's scores and keep flipping the lead late. A short Etienne touchdown reception off a swing pass in the fourth quarter came against man coverage that left him free to the edge.

But the game's biggest swing came on the other side of the ball. With Jacksonville facing a one-score deficit late in the fourth quarter, Lawrence's pass over the middle intended for Jakobi Meyers was picked off by Bishop, handing Buffalo the ball back in prime position. That turnover set up Buffalo's final answer — Allen punching a one-yard touchdown run in with barely a minute left to take the lead for good. Jacksonville also threw an earlier first-quarter interception, and lost a fumble on a kickoff return in the second quarter that gave Buffalo good field position, two more moments where the ball simply came out of Jacksonville's hands at the wrong time. In a game with this many lead changes, it was Buffalo's ability to finish — on fourth downs, in the red zone, and on that final defensive stand — that made the difference.
