This one came down to Josh Allen answering every Jacksonville punch with a bigger one, and the game finally tipping Buffalo's way in the last two minutes.

The Bills won this game through the air and with Allen's legs when it mattered most. Allen kept finding chunks against Jacksonville's zone looks — a 36-yard strike to Keon Coleman down the sideline in the second quarter, then, needing an answer late, a nearly identical 36-yard shot to Brandin Cooks on a deep post that he delivered even through pressure. That Cooks throw set up the go-ahead score minutes later, a touchdown to Dalton Kincaid out of the same shotgun look that had been gashing Jacksonville's zone all night. Buffalo's passing game was simply hitting on throws that don't always connect, and it was the separator in a game the Bills led narrowly at the finish.

On the ground, Jacksonville actually won the yardage battle — Travis Etienne broke a 26-yard run behind a light box in the third quarter, added a 12-yard conversion on third down, and Bhayshul Tuten ripped off 13 yards on the ground as well. But Buffalo's run game did its damage in the moments that counted instead of accumulating yards: Allen scored on a goal-line keeper in the second quarter to take the lead, then twice converted fourth-and-1 with the ball in his own hands, including a replay-reversed leaper at the goal line in the fourth quarter that eventually became the winning score on the very next snap. Jacksonville ran better between the twenties; Buffalo ran better in the red zone and on fourth down, and that's the version of the run game that decided things.

Buffalo also finished its trips inside the red zone with touchdowns instead of settling for stalled drives — the Allen goal-line runs and the Kincaid catch all capped drives with six points rather than three, which matters against a Jacksonville team that kept answering scores of its own.

Jacksonville's offense had success too, with Trevor Lawrence hitting Brian Thomas Jr., Parker Washington, and Etienne for touchdowns to keep pulling back ahead in the second half. But the game's biggest turning point came on defense: with under a minute left and Jacksonville trailing by three, Lawrence's short middle throw intended for Jakobi Meyers was picked off by Bishop, snuffing out the Jaguars' answer and handing Buffalo the ball back in position to close it out. Jacksonville also turned it over early when Lawrence threw an interception on a post route in the first quarter, and Buffalo lost a fumble on a kickoff return in the second quarter that briefly gave momentum back to Jacksonville — but it was that final interception, with the clock running out, that sealed the outcome.
